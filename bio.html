About

Personal notes on culture, taste and fixation.
01 / ORIGIN

The collection

I've always been interested in culture, although for a long time I understood that interest primarily through the act of collecting. As a child, I accumulated things with an almost indiscriminate enthusiasm: DVDs, video games, CDs, vinyl, magazines, books, memorabilia — anything that could be acquired, preserved, revisited or absorbed. I was fascinated by the objects themselves, but equally by the worlds contained within them. The artwork on a DVD cover, the typography of an album sleeve, the mythology surrounding a particular artist, the atmosphere of a film, the identity constructed around a football club, the aesthetics of a video game. Everything seemed to possess its own language, history and cultural significance.

That fascination never particularly subsided. What began as a childhood habit has since developed into a personal archive comprising thousands of pieces of physical media, accumulated across genres, decades and cultural movements. Somewhere between the stacks of discs, obsolete formats, forgotten releases and things acquired simply because they felt interesting, collecting became less about ownership and more about preservation — an attempt, perhaps, to retain fragments of culture that might otherwise disappear into the accelerating ephemerality of contemporary life.

I've always been drawn to the obscure, the overlooked, the out-of-print and the things that exist just outside the boundaries of what is considered culturally relevant at any given moment. The archive continues to grow, largely without any particular intention of stopping.

02 / FORMATION

Learning to look

At school, my interests were similarly difficult to confine to a single discipline. I gravitated towards English, history, art, music, drama, dance and sport — subjects that, in retrospect, all offered different ways of understanding people and the worlds they inhabit. I was interested in narrative, image, performance, identity, historical context, expression and the ways in which people communicate meaning, consciously or otherwise.

Culture was never something I encountered exclusively through the classroom. It existed in music videos, films, football terraces, television, fashion, friendships, the internet and the countless small rituals through which people construct a sense of belonging.

Eventually, these interests converged around media. I studied Media and Film at A-Level before progressing to University College London, where I studied Media with a specialism in Film Production. University gave me the opportunity to interrogate the things I had spent years instinctively gravitating towards — to consider moving image not simply as entertainment, but as a cultural artefact; to understand production as both a creative and collaborative process; and to explore the relationship between representation, authorship, audiences, technology and the wider social conditions in which media exists.

Curiosity became methodology. Observation became research. Interests became the beginnings of a practice.
03 / IN PRACTICE

From observation to production

Since then, I've found myself working across the intersections of marketing, creative strategy, production, research and digital media. I've contributed to projects spanning Google, COPA90, Barclays, Bullion Productions, Def Jam and Sony Music, with work connected to Arsenal FC, BBC Sport, ITV, Nike, UEFA and others. My experiences have brought me into proximity with people and institutions across sport, music, film and entertainment, including projects featuring England internationals Chloe Kelly and Leah Williamson, six-time MOBO Award-winning artist Stormzy, singer-songwriter Mahalia, football creator Jeremy Lynch, BAFTA-nominated filmmaker Gurinder Chadha and Sadiq Khan, Mayor of London.

Much of my work has involved existing somewhere between the initial idea and its eventual realisation — researching, developing concepts, identifying narratives, understanding audiences, producing content and translating abstract ideas into something people can actually engage with. Content I've researched and developed has generated more than one million views across platforms. I've helped a charity grow its social following by over 2,000 in under three months, contributed to securing Nike sponsorships for grassroots football, and worked on projects subsequently featured by BBC News, The Athletic, INDIVISA and England Football. In 2026, I programmed and delivered a youth-led event for the British Film Institute.

04 / SUBJECTS

Things I can't leave alone

I'm interested in the relationship between culture and behaviour. Why certain images become iconic. Why particular sounds, aesthetics or ideas resonate with one generation and alienate another. How communities form around shared obsessions. How taste is acquired, performed and legitimised. How something once considered peripheral eventually becomes commercially desirable, and how industries attempt to manufacture the very cultural relevance they spend so much time trying to understand.

The distance between underground and mainstream has always fascinated me — not least because the distinction is rarely as stable as it appears.

05 / SYSTEMS

Culture, quantified

This curiosity extends into the digital landscape, where the internet has fundamentally altered the conditions under which culture is produced, distributed, discovered and consumed. Online communities, recommendation systems, algorithms, memetics, parasocial relationships and the increasingly blurred boundaries between participation and spectatorship offer an endless amount to examine.

I'm interested in the internet not merely as technological infrastructure, but as a cultural environment in its own right: a place where identities are rehearsed, subcultures emerge, collective obsessions take shape and the distinction between the individual and the audience becomes increasingly difficult to locate.

My approach is inherently multidisciplinary. I tend to move between creative instinct and analytical inquiry, drawing upon cultural research, audience insight, marketing, visual storytelling and data to better understand the relationship between people and the media they inhabit. I've developed an interest in the more technical dimensions of that process, working with SQL, Python, Excel and Power BI, while expanding my understanding of web development through HTML, JavaScript and UX/UI design.

I'm interested in what happens when subjective cultural observation meets something more systematic — when intuition can be interrogated, patterns identified and assumptions challenged without necessarily sacrificing the ambiguity that makes culture interesting in the first place.

06 / PERSONAL ARCHIVE

Still collecting

Outside of work, I'm still doing largely what I've always done: collecting, watching, listening, reading, researching and becoming disproportionately interested in things most people would probably consider inconsequential. Cinema, particularly horror, experimental film and documentary, remains a significant part of my creative interests. So do music, football, fashion, internet culture, visual art, obscure media, forgotten histories and the strange specificity of subcultures.

My interests are less a neatly organised set of hobbies than an ever-expanding network of references, rabbit holes and things I haven't quite finished investigating.

I've always wanted to understand the things that capture people's attention — and, more importantly, what those things reveal about us. The objects we keep. The stories we repeat. The aesthetics we adopt. The communities we seek out. The things we decide are worth caring about.

Perhaps that is what collecting was always about. Not simply accumulating things, but attempting to make sense of the worlds they came from.

I'm still collecting. Only the subject has become considerably larger.
