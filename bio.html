About

Field notes on culture, creativity, technology, and the systems that connect them.
01 / ORIGIN

The collection

I've always been interested in culture, though for a long time I understood that interest primarily through collecting. As a child, I accumulated things with indiscriminate enthusiasm: DVDs, video games, CDs, vinyl, magazines, books, memorabilia, anything that could be acquired, preserved, or absorbed. I was fascinated by the objects themselves, but even more by the worlds inside them: the artwork on a DVD sleeve, the typography of an album cover, the mythology around an artist, the atmosphere of a film, the identity built around a football club.

Everything possessed its own language and cultural weight.

That habit never subsided. What started in childhood has grown into a personal archive of thousands of pieces of physical media spanning decades, genres, and movements. Somewhere between stacks of discs, obsolete formats, and forgotten releases, collecting became less about ownership and more about preservation, holding onto fragments of culture before they disappear into the feed.

I've always been drawn to the obscure, the overlooked, and whatever exists just outside the boundaries of mainstream relevance. The archive continues to grow, with no real intention of stopping.

02 / FORMATION

Learning to look

My interests have never stayed within a single discipline. I gravitated towards English, history, art, music, drama, dance, sport, and IT, different ways of understanding people, expression, identity, technology, and how meaning is communicated.

Culture was never something I encountered only in classrooms. It lived in music videos, films, football terraces, television, fashion, friendships, the internet, and the small rituals people use to build belonging.

Those interests eventually converged around media. After studying Media and Film at A-Level, I went on to study Media, specialising in Film Production, at University College London (UCL).

University gave me the tools to interrogate what I had spent years instinctively gravitating towards: treating moving image not simply as entertainment, but as a cultural artefact shaped by representation, authorship, audiences, and technology.

It also expanded how I think about creative work, not just as something to make, but something to research, contextualise, question, and understand.

Curiosity became methodology. Observation became research. Interests became the beginnings of a practice.
03 / IN PRACTICE

From observation to execution

Since graduating, I've worked across creative production, marketing, cultural research, and communications, contributing to projects spanning Google, COPA90, Barclays, Bullion Productions, Def Jam, and Sony Music, with work connected to Arsenal FC, BBC Sport, ITV, Nike, and UEFA.

Along the way, I've contributed to projects across sport, music, and film, with opportunities to work around people including England internationals Chloe Kelly and Leah Williamson, six-time MOBO Award winner Stormzy, singer-songwriter Mahalia, creator Jeremy Lynch, BAFTA-nominated filmmaker Gurinder Chadha, and Mayor of London Sadiq Khan.

My work often sits between an initial idea and its execution: researching audiences, identifying cultural narratives, developing concepts, supporting production, and translating observations into content and campaigns that connect with people.

I'm particularly interested in the relationship between creative decisions and the environments in which they are received, the audiences, communities, platforms, and cultural contexts that give work its meaning.

04 / SUBJECTS

Things I can't leave alone

I'm fascinated by the relationship between culture, identity, and behaviour: why certain images become iconic, why particular sounds or aesthetics resonate with one generation and alienate another, and how communities form around shared obsessions.

The distance between underground and mainstream has always intrigued me. I'm interested in how ideas travel, how subcultures develop, how taste is formed, and how seemingly niche interests can influence wider cultural and commercial landscapes.

I also find the relationship between creative expression and technology particularly compelling. The tools, platforms, and systems through which culture is made and consumed are constantly changing, and so are the ways people discover, interpret, and participate in it.

05 / SYSTEMS

Culture, code & technology

The internet isn't merely technological infrastructure, it's a cultural environment. A place where identities are rehearsed, subcultures emerge, communities form, and collective obsessions take shape.

Algorithms, recommendation systems, social platforms, and emerging technologies have fundamentally changed how culture is produced, discovered, distributed, and consumed. They've also created new relationships between creators, audiences, and the systems that connect them.

This is where my interests in media and technology converge.

Alongside my creative background, I'm developing my technical practice across programming, data analytics, and digital design. My current toolkit includes SQL, Python, C, Excel, Power BI, HTML, JavaScript, and UX/UI design.

I'm interested in using these disciplines to investigate questions that sit between culture and technology: understanding audience behaviour through data, exploring digital communities through research, experimenting with interactive media, and using code to build or test ideas.

I don't see analytical and creative thinking as opposing approaches. I'm interested in what happens when they inform one another, when cultural intuition meets systematic investigation, when data reveals patterns worth exploring, and when technology becomes a medium for creative expression rather than simply a tool for efficiency.

06 / PERSONAL ARCHIVE

Still collecting

Outside of work, I'm still collecting, watching, listening, reading, and becoming disproportionately invested in things most people overlook.

Cinema, particularly horror, experimental film, and documentary, sits at the centre of that, alongside music, football, fashion, internet folklore, and forgotten histories.

I'm interested in the worlds people build around the things they love: the references, aesthetics, rituals, communities, and shared languages that turn an interest into an identity.

My interests are less a neatly organised set of hobbies than an ongoing field study in what makes people care.

07 / PRESENT

What comes next

I'm interested in work that brings creative, cultural, and technical disciplines together.

That could mean exploring audience behaviour through research and analytics, contributing to creative and brand strategy, developing ideas through production, or experimenting with the possibilities of programming and emerging technologies.

I'm not particularly interested in confining that ambition to one discipline or job title. I want to work across the spaces where media, culture, creativity, and technology meet, contributing to projects that are intellectually curious, creatively ambitious, and meaningful to the people they reach.

I'm always interested in connecting with people, organisations, and projects exploring similar territory.
