01 / ORIGIN

The collection

I've always been interested in culture, though for a long time I understood that interest primarily through collecting. As a child, I accumulated things with indiscriminate enthusiasm: DVDs, video games, CDs, vinyl, magazines, books, memorabilia, anything that could be acquired, preserved, or absorbed. I was fascinated by the objects themselves, but even more by the worlds inside them: the artwork on a DVD sleeve, the typography of an album cover, the mythology around an artist, the atmosphere of a film, the identity built around a football club. Everything possessed its own language and cultural weight.

That habit never subsided. What started in childhood has grown into a personal archive of thousands of pieces of physical media spanning decades, genres, and movements. Somewhere between stacks of discs, obsolete formats, and forgotten releases, collecting became less about ownership and more about preservation, holding onto fragments of culture before they vanish into the feed.

I've always been drawn to the obscure, the overlooked, and whatever exists just outside the boundaries of mainstream relevance. The archive continues to grow, with no real intention of stopping.

02 / FORMATION

Learning to look

At school, my interests refused to stay inside a single discipline. I gravitated toward English, history, art, music, drama, dance, and sport, all different lenses for understanding people and how they communicate meaning, consciously or otherwise.

Culture was never something I encountered only in classrooms. It lived in music videos, films, football terraces, television, fashion, friendships, the internet, and the small rituals people use to build belonging.

Those instincts eventually converged around media. After studying Media and Film at A-Level, I studied Media, specialising in Film Production, at University College London (UCL). University gave me the tools to interrogate what I had spent years instinctively gravitating toward: treating moving image not just as entertainment, but as a cultural artefact shaped by representation, authorship, audiences, and technology.

Curiosity became methodology. Observation became research. Interests became the beginnings of a practice.
03 / IN PRACTICE

From observation to production

Since graduating, I've worked across the intersections of marketing, creative strategy, cultural research, and production, contributing to projects spanning Google, COPA90, Barclays, Bullion Productions, Def Jam, and Sony Music, with work connected to Arsenal FC, BBC Sport, ITV, Nike, and UEFA. Along the way, my work has brought me close to leading voices across sport, music, and film, including England internationals Chloe Kelly and Leah Williamson, six-time MOBO Award winner Stormzy, singer-songwriter Mahalia, creator Jeremy Lynch, BAFTA-nominated filmmaker Gurinder Chadha, and Mayor of London Sadiq Khan.

Much of my work sits between the initial spark and its final execution, researching audiences, spotting cultural narratives, shaping concepts, and translating abstract insights into campaigns people actually care about.

04 / SUBJECTS

Things I can't leave alone

I'm fascinated by the relationship between culture and behaviour: why certain images become iconic, why particular sounds or aesthetics resonate with one generation and alienate another, and how communities form around shared obsessions.

The distance between underground and mainstream has always intrigued me, not least because the boundary is rarely as stable as it looks. For brands, that's where the real strategic advantage lives: spotting cultural shifts early enough to show up with genuine relevance rather than chasing trends six months too late.

05 / SYSTEMS

Culture, quantified

The internet isn't merely technological infrastructure; it's a cultural environment where identities are rehearsed, subcultures emerge, and collective obsessions take shape. Algorithms, recommendation systems, and digital communities have fundamentally changed how culture is discovered and consumed, blurring the line between spectator and participant.

My approach moves deliberately between creative instinct and analytical inquiry, combining cultural research and visual storytelling with SQL, Python, Excel, Power BI, HTML, JavaScript, and UX/UI design. I'm interested in what happens when subjective cultural observation meets systematic data: when intuition is tested and patterns are uncovered without stripping away the ambiguity that makes culture compelling in the first place.

06 / PERSONAL ARCHIVE

Still collecting

Outside of work, I'm still collecting, watching, listening, reading, and becoming disproportionately invested in things most people overlook. Cinema, especially horror, experimental film, and documentary, sits at the centre of that, alongside music, football, fashion, internet folklore, and forgotten histories.

My interests are less a neatly organised set of hobbies than an ongoing field study in what makes people care.

07 / PRESENT

What comes next

I'm interested in opportunities and collaborations across brand and creative strategy, cultural research, audience insights, and production, particularly where a strong understanding of culture can shape work with real relevance.
