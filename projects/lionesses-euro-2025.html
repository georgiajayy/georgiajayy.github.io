Third Time’s a Charm?

Lionesses · Euro 2025

Spain created more than twice England’s chances in the Euro 2025 final. England still lifted the trophy. I wanted to know how, and whether they can make it three in a row in 2029.

THE FINAL

Out-created. Still won.

THE QUESTION

What does the data suggest about a third straight title at Euro 2029 in Germany?

WHAT THE DATA SAYS

  1. England grew into the tournament, then survived

    Out-created by France in the opener, dominant against the Netherlands and Wales, then out-created again in the final, and still won on penalties.

  2. Volume, not quality

    Spain took 23 shots to England’s 8, but shot for shot England’s chances were just as good.

  3. Penalties distorted the raw numbers

    With penalties removed, Russo and Toone led England’s open-play threat.

  4. Spain are rising

    England win through clinical finishing rather than dominance, and have allowed more chances at every tournament since 2019.

  5. England’s attack is heading towards 30

    9 of 17 attacking contributors will be 30+ by Euro 2029, accounting for 49.8% of attacking involvement.

THE VERDICT

MY CALL

Not on current trends.

England won Euro 2025 through finishing, not control, and that gap has widened every tournament since 2019. With almost half their attacking output coming from players who’ll be 30+ by 2029, a third straight title depends on the next generation arriving in time. Charm alone won’t do it.

LOOKING BACK

The most valuable moments came from the data correcting me. My first read of the final’s shot map was that Spain had better chances; the numbers showed they simply had more of them. Checking visuals against numbers is now a habit.

The analysis has honest limits: four tournaments is a short trend, some samples are small, and it describes what happened rather than predicting what will. Next, I’d like to use WSL data to find young English players performing well at club level, and build my own expected goals model.
