Can AI Pundit the Game?

Matchday Social Desk · Microsoft × Premier League

Fans don’t want the data. They want the story. So for the Inside the Game hackathon, I built a team of AI agents that watches a match live and tells it differently for every fan.

  • ✣ ROLEIdea, design, build + video
  • ✣ BUILT INTwo days
  • ✣ DATAA synthetic match I generated
  • ✣ TOOLSPython · Streamlit · Microsoft Foundry

THE BRIEF

Every match, made for every fan.

Microsoft and the Premier League asked developers to turn football data into match intelligence, live narratives and recaps that work for studios, streaming and fans in any market. I saw it on a Monday. Two days later, Matchday Social Desk was submitted.

THE MATCH

Wonders 2, Eagles 0.

HOW IT WORKS

  1. The Analyst explains why it matters

    A live stats engine tracks momentum, xG, pressing and a control-vs-chaos index, and flags the moments that matter. The Analyst works out what’s really going on.

  2. Fan Voice writes it for you

    Analysts get the numbers. Casual fans get plain English. Pick a club, a player, or follow one player all game.

  3. The Fact-checker sends it back

    Any number that isn’t in the match data gets the caption rejected and rewritten. Fail twice and you get a plain, safe caption built straight from the numbers.

  4. The Translator takes it global

    8 languages, with names and numbers kept exactly as they are.

  5. The Pundit answers back

    At half-time, ask it anything. It looks up the stats before it answers, and it can’t see the future, so no spoilers.

  6. The Social Desk makes the posts

    Every big moment becomes a ready-to-post package for the club’s channels: a hook, a caption, a format idea and a branded graphic.

THE RULE

MY RULE

If fans can’t trust it, it’s useless.

An AI that invents a stat on a live broadcast is worse than no AI at all. So nothing reaches the screen until it has been checked against the data, every agent step is logged, and each caption says whether it was fact-checked.

LOOKING BACK

The hardest part wasn’t getting the AI to write. It was stopping it writing things that weren’t true, and stopping it from spoiling the ending: each moment only ever sees the match as it stood at that minute.

The project has honest limits: the match is synthetic, the events are replayed rather than streamed, and nothing is read from video yet. Next, I’d like to plug it into a real-time event feed and test it on real fans.
